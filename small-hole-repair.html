How to Fix a Small Hole in a Fiberglass Boat

Finding a small hole or gouge in your boat's hull can be alarming, but the good news is that fiberglass is incredibly repairable. Whether the hole is the size of a coin or a few inches wide, you can fix it yourself with the right materials and a little patience.

Step 1: Assess the Damage

First, determine if the hole is purely cosmetic (only in the gelcoat) or structural (penetrating the fiberglass layers). If you can see the glass fibers or the hole goes all the way through the hull, it is a structural repair. If it's just a chip in the smooth outer layer, it's cosmetic.

Step 2: Bevel the Edges (The Crucial Step)

You cannot just fill a hole like a pothole. You must taper the edges outward. Using a grinder with a 36-grit disc, grind the area into a shallow "V" or bowl shape. The rule of thumb is a 12:1 taper ratio—for every 1mm of thickness, you need to grind back 12mm. This allows the new fiberglass to bond mechanically and overlap smoothly.

Step 3: Clean the Surface

Wipe down the entire ground area with acetone. This removes all dust, wax, and oils. The surface must be perfectly clean for the resin to adhere.

Step 4: Layering the Fiberglass

For small holes, the best material is chopped strand mat (CSM) or a fine woven cloth. You will need multiple layers. Cut your cloth to size, starting with the smallest piece for the bottom of the hole and getting progressively larger.

  1. Apply a layer of mixed epoxy to the hole using a brush.
  2. Place the smallest piece of fiberglass cloth into the hole.
  3. Apply more resin and press the cloth down with a roller or brush to remove air bubbles.
  4. Repeat with the next larger layer, overlapping the edges.
  5. Continue until the repair is slightly higher than the surrounding hull.

Step 5: Fairing and Sanding

Once the epoxy is fully cured (usually 24 hours), you must sand it smooth. Use an angle grinder or a random orbital sander to bring the repair down flush with the hull. Apply a fairing compound (epoxy mixed with microballoons) to fill any low spots, let it cure, and sand again.

Step 6: Gelcoat or Paint

Because epoxy is not UV resistant, you must cover the repair. If the repair is above the waterline, you can apply a matching gelcoat or marine polyurethane paint. For below the waterline, use an epoxy primer followed by anti-fouling paint.
