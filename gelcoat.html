Gelcoat Repair Guide: Fixing Scratches and Cracks

Gelcoat is the shiny, protective outer layer of your fiberglass boat. It looks great, but it is susceptible to scratches, gouges, and spider cracks (crazing). Learning to repair gelcoat yourself can save you hundreds of dollars in professional labor.

Gelcoat vs. Paint: Which should you use?

If your gelcoat is faded or damaged, you have two options: reapply gelcoat or paint the boat.

  • Gelcoat: This is the original factory finish. It is incredibly durable, UV resistant, and long-lasting. However, it is difficult to apply (requires a catalyst and precise mixing) and requires a mold-release agent to cure properly, making it hard for DIYers to get a perfect finish. It is best for small, localized repairs.
  • Marine Paint: Modern two-part polyurethane paints (like Awlgrip or Interlux) are much easier to apply than gelcoat. They offer excellent UV protection and a high-gloss finish. If you are painting the entire hull or large sections, marine paint is usually the better choice for a DIYer.

How to Repair Gelcoat Scratches

If the scratch is shallow and hasn't penetrated the fiberglass, you can often fix it with a gelcoat repair kit. Here is the process:

  1. Clean the area: Wipe the scratch with acetone to remove wax and dirt.
  2. Bevel the edges: Use a small pick or sandpaper to slightly widen the scratch, creating a "V" shape that will hold the new gelcoat.
  3. Mix the gelcoat: Mix your gelcoat paste with the hardener (usually MEKP). You can also add a coloring agent to match your boat's original color.
  4. Apply: Use a small brush or a syringe to fill the scratch with the gelcoat paste. Overfill it slightly so it sits proud of the surface.
  5. Cover and Cure: Cover the repair with clear tape or a special mold-release film. This blocks the air so the gelcoat cures properly. Wait for it to harden.
  6. Sand and Polish: Once cured, wet-sand the excess gelcoat flush with the surrounding surface (start with 400-grit, then move to 800, then 1500). Finally, use a rubbing compound to restore the shine.

How to Repair Gelcoat Spider Cracks

Spider cracks (or crazing) are small, web-like cracks in the gelcoat. They are usually caused by stress or impact. To fix them, you must grind out the cracks completely (down to the fiberglass) with a Dremel tool, clean with acetone, fill with thickened epoxy, and then apply a new layer of gelcoat.

How much does a gelcoat repair cost?

A professional gelcoat repair can cost anywhere from $150 to $500 for a small area, depending on the size and the shop's hourly rate. A DIY gelcoat repair kit typically costs between $30 and $80 and contains enough materials for multiple repairs.
